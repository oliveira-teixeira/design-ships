## Overview

TAPP is a social network built around privacy, with shopping inside the feed. The investor pitch aimed it at moms and dads and their circles, starting with women aged 27 to 65 on the Westside of Los Angeles. Brands open storefronts inside the feed and public figures review products in depth, while users can opt into an assistant that turns product mentions in their posts into suggestions. I designed the parts of the product where money moves. I also built the two decks TAPP used to present the idea, an investors' round pitch and a feature concept pitch.

## Challenge

The investor deck opens on the problem: public feeds full of stalking, bullying, bots, impersonators and deep fakes. TAPP asked people to trust a social app with more than their photos. Users would keep a balance in the app, send gift cards to friends and family, and buy from brands they follow. So a social product had to carry card details, balances and purchase history. The payment screens needed the calm and clarity of a banking app. Brands also needed a paid way to be seen that would not turn the feed back into the ad space users were trying to leave.

## Approach

Virtual card system. I modeled the wallet on banking apps people already trust: the balance at the top, quick actions to add, send, receive and save, a card carousel, and a searchable history of payments and invoices. A prompt invites users to set up a virtual credit card as an extra layer of security, so their real card never travels through the social side of the app. Gift cards move peer to peer between friends and family.

Commerce inside the feed. Brands tag products in posts, friends recommend and rate them, and checkout happens without leaving the conversation.

Bid Your Spotlight. Brands bid for spotlight placements in the feed, so paid visibility follows brand demand and stays clearly marked.

Privacy cues. Visibility filters, clear signals about what is shared, and payment screens that show only the data each step needs.

## Outcome

The work produced the mobile app design and two decks I built for TAPP. The investors' round pitch covers market sizing and a revenue model based on a 15 percent commission on brand sales with no inventory. The feature concept pitch tells the product story through its hero screens. On the product side, the key pieces are the Neo Balance interface for the wallet, the Bid Your Spotlight ad format and a checkout that lives inside social posts. The deck planned Shop with Friends for Q3 2024 and the Shopping Assistant for Q4 2024.
