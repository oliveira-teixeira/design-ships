## Overview

Vintage Cards runs a website and an app for vintage sports card collectors, a small audience that knows its cards in detail. Collectors use it to track progress on a set, search the card database, check graded values and scan cards with their phone. The client came to Fetchly with a focused goal: improve specific features in the app and website for a growing community. The brand is loud on purpose, with script logos, halftone color and vintage card art, and every change had to fit it.

## Challenge

Vintage Cards already had a loyal audience and a recognizable identity, and the client wanted to keep both. The goal was to improve usability in specific features of the app and website without disrupting the structure and character collectors are used to. That meant first finding where the experience broke down, separating what worked from what did not, and agreeing on priorities with the client before changing any screen. The redesign ended up concentrated on the card finder, the set pages and the mobile layouts.

## Approach

We started with product discovery. A flow map organized the platform into three layers, card intelligence and discovery, the personal hub, and community and social, and a board of research notes and user quotes sat next to it.

From there we set a dated roadmap. Week one covered product definition and a strategic UX foundation. Weeks two and three covered interface audits, component redesigns, and visual and usability improvements in the key user flows. Week four covered design QA and mobile adjustments, with a fifth week held open.

The redesigned screens keep the Vintage Cards look. A card finder lets collectors search by player, team, year or set, or filter by sport, year and set. Set pages show progress with checked and remaining cards, a gallery or list view, and sorting by grading and value. On mobile, the same finder and set tracking fit a single column.

## Outcome

The engagement delivered the discovery board, a dated roadmap running from May 12 to June 13, 2025, and redesigned desktop and mobile screens for the home page, the card finder and the set pages, reviewed in a design QA pass. Every redesigned screen keeps the existing logo, palette and card imagery, so the changes read as improvements to a product collectors already know.
